# DL-Project
Understanding and classifying social behaviors in animals is a fundamental requirement in behavioral neuroscience to effectively capture complex behavioral patterns in a real-world research environment. This paper presents a deep learning based pipeline for automatic recognition of social behaviors in mice using pose estimation data. The proposed system combines convolutional feature extraction, temporal attention modeling, and multi agent interaction analysis to classify behaviors such as attack, chase, and mount. The approach consists of a Variational AutoEncoder (VAE) for unsupervised latent representation learning, which works on both labelled and unlabeled data. The latent representations are then fed into a dual-head classification model, which separately predicts solo and pair actions of mice, enabling precise detection of both individual and interactive behaviors. Experimental results show that the proposed method consistently outperforms baseline models including Transformer and CNN-based models across major evaluation metrics. This improvement is primarily due to the unsupervised  training of the encoder, which helps work with large amounts of unlabelled behavioral data. Overall, it was concluded that the proposed Variational AutoEncoder-based learning along with the classifier model approach offers a robust and scalable solution for automated social behavior recognition in mice.
